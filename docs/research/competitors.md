# Competitors: FDA-cleared and CE-marked chest X-ray AI

> **For research use only. Not for diagnostic use.**
> Due-diligence research for the Second Reader project. Performance numbers are transcribed from FDA 510(k) summaries (primary) or peer-reviewed papers; company marketing numbers are labelled as such. All URLs accessed 2026-09-21. "n/a" means not stated in the source we could read, not that it does not exist.

## 1. How the US regulatory categories map to our product

| Product code / regulation | What it is | Special-control evidence bar (as applied in the summaries below) |
|---|---|---|
| **QFM / QAS — 21 CFR 892.2080** "radiological computer-aided triage and notification" (CADt) | Flags a study in the worklist; no marks on the image; must not remove cases; "passive notification" | Standalone AUC > 0.95 with sensitivity and specificity > 80% (lower CI bounds cited), plus a turnaround-time bench study. No reader study required. [1][2][3] |
| **MYN — 21 CFR 892.2070** "medical image analyzer" (CADe) | Draws boxes/marks on a copy of the image as a concurrent or second-read aid | Standalone performance **and** a fully-crossed MRMC study showing aided > unaided (AUC or wAFROC). [4][5] |
| CE MDR Class IIb | EU; most comprehensive CXR products (Annalise 124 findings, Oxipit autonomous normal reporting) sit here | Notified-body review; no public summaries equivalent to 510(k). |

Second Reader (probability + heat-map + box for three findings, concurrent/second read) is a **CADe/MYN**-type product, so the Gleamer, Qure qXR-LN/qXR-Detect and AZmed Rayvolve LN clearances are the closest regulatory comparators, not the triage products.

## 3. Which vendors publish subgroup or external-site performance (our differentiator)

- **In the 510(k) summary itself:** Qure.ai (qXR-PTX-PE: AUC by sex and age; qXR-LN: age, sex, race, manufacturer, view, confounders) and Annalise (race/ethnicity composition and demographic stratification, manufacturer/model counts) are the only two that describe subgroup analysis in public summaries. Aidoc reports manufacturer mix but says ethnicity was unavailable. Lunit, Vuno, Behold and Zebra summaries have no subgroup tables. [1][2][3][4][15][29][36][38][41]
- **Independent external validation** (not vendor-run) exists for Lunit, Annalise, Oxipit, Siemens, Milvue and, in aggregate, seven unnamed lung-cancer devices (Maiter 2026). It is generally less flattering than the 510(k) numbers (§4).
- **Nobody** we found publishes calibration (ECE), per-site drift, or a locked external test that customers can re-run. That is the gap Second Reader's principles 1, 2 and 8 target.

## 4. What independent studies say (the honest baseline)

| Study | Design | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Plesner et al., *Radiology* 2023;308:e231236 — four commercial tools (Annalise Enterprise CXR 2.2, Milvue SmartUrgencies 1.24, Oxipit ChestEye 2.6, Siemens AI-Rad Companion 10) [26] | 2,040 consecutive patients, 4 Danish hospitals, thoracic-radiologist reference | AUC 0.89–0.97 PTX, 0.94–0.97 effusion; sensitivity 63–90% PTX, 62–95% effusion; sensitivity for **small** PTX/effusion 9–94% vs 81–100% for large; specificity fell to 65–92% (effusion) when ≥4 findings present; "more false-positive findings than radiology reports". |
| Kim et al., *Eur Radiol* 2023;33:3501 — commercial CXR AI in low-prevalence screening [10] | 3,047 CXRs, 2 Korean primary-care centres, CT reference, 2.2% prevalence | AI AUROC **0.648**, Se 35.3%, Sp 94.2%; "may not directly translate to real-world practice, especially where the demand for AI assistance is highest". |
| Maiter et al., *Radiology* 2026;319:e252205 — seven commercial devices, lung cancer [47] | 5,235 primary-care CXRs, single UK centre, 1.4% cancer prevalence, MDT reference | AUC 0.80–0.94; sensitivity 20.8–77.8%; PPV 1.5–28.4%; extra false positives vs radiologist 10 to 2,039; inter-device agreement κ = 0.24. |
| Farquhar, medRxiv 2026.01.20.26344495 — five DenseNet models on RSNA + NIH [48] | Variance decomposition | Radiograph **view (AP vs PA)** explained 87% (RSNA) and 69% (NIH) of performance variance; PA miss rates 30–78%; age 5–30%, sex <2%. |
| Seyyed-Kalantari et al., *Nat Med* 2021;27:2176 [49] | MIMIC-CXR, CheXpert, ChestX-ray14 classifiers | Classifiers "consistently and selectively underdiagnosed under-served patient populations", worst for intersectional groups (e.g., Hispanic female). |
| Woznitza et al., LungIMPACT RCT, *Nat Med* 2026 [50] | 93,326 CXRs, multicentre NHS, AI prioritisation randomised by day | No change in time to CT (ratio 0.97) or lung-cancer diagnosis (0.98). |

## 5. Post-market problems and recalls

- We searched FDA recall/press listings and trade press for each product above and found **no product-specific recall or MAUDE-linked event** for any chest X-ray AI product. This is a negative search result, not proof of absence; the FDA recall database (https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cdrh/cfdocs/cfres/res.cfm) and MAUDE should be queried directly by manufacturer name before a pitch.
- Sector-level: Muehlematter et al. (*Lancet Digit Health* 2023) found that AI/ML tasks "changed frequently along the device's predicate network, raising safety concerns" and that "only a few recalls might have affected the AI/ML components". [51] A 2026 *npj Digit Med* analysis of 956 radiology AI devices (1995–2025) reported that 44.87% had software version updates and 124 recall reports were attributed to software defects *(secondary; article page not retrievable)*. [52]
- Commercial failure is the more common post-market problem: Behold.ai administration (2025), Zebra's sale for a fraction of its raise (2021), Oxipit absorbed by Sectra (2026). [39][42][22]
